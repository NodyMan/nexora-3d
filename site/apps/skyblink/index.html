See the sky move.

SkyBlink takes real infrared images from NASA’s SPHEREx telescope, lines up visits taken months apart and blinks them. Stars stay still. What moves, jumps, and SkyBlink measures by how much.

How it works

The trick that found Pluto, in your browser.

In 1930 astronomers flipped between two photos of the same sky to spot what moved. SkyBlink does the same with today’s telescope data, and does the maths for you.

01

Pick a target

Type a star name or coordinates, or start with one of five objects from the challenge. A Simple mode keeps it easy for first-timers.

02

Blink the visits

SkyBlink downloads SPHEREx image cutouts, puts every visit on one pixel grid and plays them as an animation. Moving objects jump; fixed stars stay put.

03

Measure the shift

Subtracting one visit from another leaves a black-and-white pair: where it was and where it is now. SkyBlink measures the shift with an error bar and checks it against the catalogue.

Honest numbers

We only claim what we measured.

Every number comes from real SPHEREx images, and every shift comes with its error. When the data can’t answer, the app says so.

Built with

A Django backend with Astropy and reproject fetches the images from NASA/IPAC IRSA and measures the motion. The front end is Next.js with an Aladin Lite sky map.
